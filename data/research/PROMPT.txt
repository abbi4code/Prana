You are researching Indian (and commonly eaten) foods for "Prana", a calorie tracker for Indian users. Your output is JSON that gets merged into the app's food database after automatic checks. Accuracy and traceability matter more than quantity: every number must come from a source row you actually opened, never from memory or estimation.

## How your output is checked (so you know what matters)
We re-read every number from the source using the reference you give (INDB food code, IFCT code, USDA fdcId, label URL, or the recipe for DERIVED items). If your numbers don't match the source row, the food is rejected. So: exact references first, numbers copied exactly as the source gives them.

## Allowed sources, in this order of preference
1. **INDB** (Indian Nutrient Databank, Anuvaad 2024): the INDB.xlsx file from github.com/lindsayjaacks/Indian-Nutrient-Databank-INDB- (sheet "Nutrient Data", ~1,014 cooked Indian recipes; columns food_code, food_name, energy_kcal, protein_g, carb_g, fat_g, fibre_g, servings_unit, unit_serving_energy_kcal). Use it for cooked Indian dishes. Download the file and read the rows, don't guess codes.
2. **IFCT 2017** (ICMR-NIN): raw ingredients, fruits, dairy, nuts, meats, fish. Read via the npm package @ifct2017/compositions (or its GitHub data). Energy in IFCT is kJ: convert kcal = kJ ÷ 4.184 and say so. Pure fats (oils, ghee) show 0 kJ in that package: use them anyway; energy = 9 kcal per g of fat (we do the same).
3. **USDA FoodData Central** (fdc.nal.usda.gov): SR Legacy or Foundation for single foods; Survey (FNDDS) for mixed/fast foods (pizza, burger, fries, ice cream). Always give the fdcId. You can read foods via the FDC API (api.nal.usda.gov/fdc/v1/food/{fdcId}?api_key=DEMO_KEY) or the food-details page. USDA "portions" (e.g. "1/2 cup = 66 g") are valid unit weights.
   **If you can't open FDC** (it's often blocked for you), still include the food: "source": { "id": "USDA", "ref": "search: <the exact FoodData Central description, e.g. 'Potato, french fries, from fresh, fried' or 'Ice cream, vanilla'>" }, "per_100g": null. We fetch the numbers ourselves and match the description exactly (FNDDS, SR Legacy, Foundation); if it doesn't match we get the closest names back. Don't use it for anything INDB or IFCT has.
4. **Official manufacturer label / official chain nutrition page** for packaged or chain foods (e.g. Amul, Mother Dairy, Kwality Wall's, Haldiram's, Bikaji, Britannia, Parle, McDonald's India, Domino's India, KFC India, Subway India). Only the brand's own site, its official nutrition PDF, or the pack image hosted by the brand. Give the exact URL and, if the numbers are in an image, the image URL.
5. **DERIVED**: a dish computed from ingredients that each come from sources 1–4, when no source has the dish (e.g. chicken biryani, air-fried chicken with 1 tsp oil, combo plates). Give the full recipe (see schema). Cooked weight (yield) must be stated with its basis; if the yield is your assumption, say "assumed" and set confidence "low".

**Never allowed** (the food is rejected if you use them, even to "confirm"): HealthifyMe, FatSecret, MyFitnessPal, Nutritionix, CalorieKing, Cronometer, Edamam, recipe blogs or their nutrition widgets, Wikipedia numbers, AI-generated nutrition sites, news articles, "average" values from any aggregator. If a dish is only on those, put it in not_found.

## Rules
- Values are **per 100 g** (per 100 ml ≈ per 100 g for drinks; say so in notes). Unknown values are null, never 0. Never round away the source's precision beyond 2 decimals.
- **Macro check:** 4 × protein + 4 × carbs + 9 × fat must be within ±15 % of kcal. If a sourced row fails it, keep kcal, set protein/carbs/fat to null, confidence "low", and say why in notes.
- **Deep-fried foods (samosa, pakora, puri, golgappa shells, bhatura, vada, bonda, jalebi, papdi, sev, bhujia, fried momos…): never send the INDB row.** INDB counts the whole pan of frying oil (bread pakora 711 kcal/100 g), so those rows are refused. Send them as DERIVED with a "frying" block instead (schema below), built like this:
  - ingredients = the dough / batter / filling BEFORE frying, each from INDB / IFCT / USDA, no frying oil;
  - fat: for the anchor study you DON'T need to open the paper (it's often blocked for you): set "fat_ref": "JPS2024:<food>:<temp>:<cycle>" with food = poori | bread pakora | mathri | french fries | potato chips, temp = 160 | 180 (poori, bread pakora and chips were fried at 180; mathri at 160; fries at both), cycle = 1 | 16 | 32, and leave fat_pct null. We hold that table (Jain, Passi & Selvamurthy, J Food Sci Technol 2024, total fat on DRY weight). For any OTHER study, give fat_pct, fat_basis (dry | as_eaten) and fat_source with the table and row;
  - use the 1st-cycle (fresh oil) value for home-made food and the 32nd-cycle (reused oil) value for street / shop food, and say which;
  - for other foods, look for a study that measured that food; otherwise use the closest measured food of the same type (wheat dough → poori or mathri; besan batter → bread pakora; potato → fries or chips) and name the proxy in notes;
  - moisture_pct = water in the fried food as eaten, from a study of that food (the same paper gives some); if none exists, give your best figure, mark moisture_source "assumed" and confidence "low".
  We compute: fat as eaten = fat_pct × (1 − moisture) on dry basis; protein / carbs / fiber fill the rest of the dry matter in the dough's own proportions. Don't send per_100g for these (null); we compute it. Set confidence "low" and start notes with "Fried (absorbed-oil model). ".
- **INDB serving sizes** are sometimes whole-recipe yields (a "plate" of 600 g). If the serving implies over ~1,000 kcal or is not one person's portion, don't use it as a unit; add a standard unit instead.
- **Units** (household measures, grams per 1 unit): use these ids when they fit: katori (150 ml small bowl), bowl (250 ml), plate, glass (250 ml), cup (150 ml), tbsp (15 ml), tsp (5 ml), handful (~30 g), pack (label serve), scoop, slice. For countable items name the unit after the thing: "puri", "piece", "roll", "momo", "tikki", "bhatura", "ladoo", "scoop" (ice cream). Each unit's "basis" says where the grams come from: "INDB serving size (derived: per-serving kcal / per-100g kcal x 100)", "USDA portion: <measure text>", "label serving size", "recipe" or "estimated standard household measure". Estimated is allowed for weights (not for nutrients); say so.
- **One food per real dish.** Variations that differ meaningfully get their own entry (chicken breast air-fried vs thigh air-fried vs tandoori chicken; vanilla vs chocolate ice cream; plain vs butter naan). Don't split trivially.
- **Recipe quantities** (how much oil for shallow-frying a tikki, garnish, water) may come from a standard recipe: say which recipe or "assumed", and use confidence "low". Only the nutrient values per ingredient must be sourced.
- **Combo plates** (chole bhature, rajma chawal, idli sambar, vada pav, pav bhaji plate) may be DERIVED from parts that are existing foods or new foods in the same batch: an ingredient with "source": { "id": "FOOD", "ref": "<that food's id>" }. List the part before the combo that uses it.
- **Aliases:** how Indians actually search and say it: Hinglish, regional names, common spellings, Hindi transliteration (e.g. pani puri, golgappa, gol gappe, puchka, phuchka, gupchup, pani batashe, pani ke batashe). No generic words that fit many foods ("curry", "sabzi", "sweet"). Lowercase. 3–10 per food.
- **name_hi:** the Hindi name in Devanagari (or the common Indian name in Devanagari).
- **id:** lowercase kebab-case ASCII, unique, stable (e.g. "chicken-biryani", "air-fried-chicken-breast", "pani-puri").
- **Don't repeat anything in the EXISTING FOODS list** at the end (same dish, same source row). If you think an existing food is missing a common name, put it in alias_suggestions instead.
- **Check before you write:** open every source row for this batch first, then write. If a page won't open, don't fill from memory: put it in not_found with the reason.
