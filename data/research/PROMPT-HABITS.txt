You are researching the health impact of smoking, other tobacco and alcohol for "Prana", a calorie and fitness tracker for Indian users. We are adding an opt-in, private "Habits" section: people log cigarettes / bidis / other tobacco each day (alcohol is already logged as drinks), and the app shows a ROUGH, sourced estimate of what that does to their body, and what improves when they cut down or quit. The numbers are computed on the phone from the formulas and tables you find. They don't need to be precise for one person, but every number must come from a source you actually opened, never from memory, and must be shown with its range. Your output is JSON that we check by re-opening every source.

## What we know about each user (the possible inputs)
- Profile: sex, age, height (cm), weight (kg), activity level. Waist (cm) and other tape measurements if they logged them. Daily calorie intake, workouts (minutes, sets), weekly exercise minutes.
- Tobacco (new, logged per day): cigarettes, bidis, hookah sessions, vape sessions, smokeless tobacco (gutka, khaini, zarda, paan with tobacco) portions. We can also ask once: age they started, years smoked, previous quit attempts, date of the last cigarette.
- Alcohol (already logged): grams of ethanol per day and per week, number of drinks per occasion (binge days), alcohol-free days.
- We do NOT have lab values (cholesterol, blood sugar), spirometry or genetics. We could ask for blood pressure (home monitor) and resting heart rate if a model really needs them: say so.

## What we want: models, not opinions
For each thing we could show, find a MODEL: inputs → formula or lookup table → output, with the published coefficients and uncertainty. Examples of the kind of output we want to show (only if a source supports it):
- "About X years of life expectancy lost at your current level (range A–B)", "each cigarette ≈ N minutes".
- "Your risk of a heart attack is about X times a non-smoker's"; "10-year heart attack / stroke risk ≈ X %"; "heart age ≈ N".
- Pack-years and what they mean (screening thresholds).
- "Since your last cigarette: …" recovery milestones, and how excess risk falls with years since quitting.
- Alcohol: risk vs grams per week, blood pressure change per drink, liver, cancer, sleep, calories.
If a source only gives a relative risk, say so: we will present it as "about X times", never as a personal probability.

## The most important output: % more risk per disease as the daily amount goes up
The screen we want most is a per-disease list that moves with the user's own daily numbers, so they see the effect on their body and are nudged to cut down:
- "Heart attack risk: about +X % vs a non-smoker at your 8 cigarettes a day (range A–B %). At 4 a day it would be about +Y %."
- "Each extra cigarette a day ≈ +Z % risk of <disease>" (only if the source gives a per-cigarette or per-band slope).
- "Cutting from 10 to 5 a day lowers your <disease> risk by about W %" and "quitting for N years brings it down to about V %" (H3).
- The same for alcohol per extra 10 g a day (about one small drink) and for bidi / smokeless tobacco where data exists.
For EVERY disease below, give the dose-response in computable form: relative risk (or hazard / odds ratio, say which) at each dose band (never smoked = 1.0; e.g. 1–4, 5–9, 10–14, 15–19, 20–24, 25+ cigarettes a day, or per pack-year), or a per-unit slope / curve equation with its confidence interval. We convert RR to "% more" ourselves (RR 1.6 = +60 %); give the RR as printed. Also give the risk for former smokers by years since quitting where it exists.
Diseases and conditions (skip any with no dose-response evidence, and list it in not_found):
- Heart and vessels: heart attack / coronary heart disease, stroke (ischaemic and haemorrhagic), peripheral artery disease, aortic aneurysm, high blood pressure, atrial fibrillation (alcohol).
- Lungs: lung cancer, COPD, asthma worsening, tuberculosis (important in India: smoking and TB infection, disease and death), pneumonia.
- Cancers: mouth, throat, oesophagus, stomach, pancreas, bladder, kidney, liver, colorectal, cervix; breast (alcohol).
- Metabolic and other: type 2 diabetes, fatty liver and cirrhosis (alcohol), erectile dysfunction, fertility, cataract and macular degeneration, gum disease and tooth loss, bone fractures / osteoporosis, skin ageing, depression / anxiety, dementia, all-cause death.
Put these in the batch where the product belongs (cigarettes in H1, bidi / smokeless in H2, quitting in H3, alcohol in H4), one model per disease (id like "smoking-dose-chd", "alcohol-dose-breast-cancer").

## Allowed sources
Peer-reviewed papers (PubMed / PMC / journal site: Lancet, BMJ, NEJM, JAMA, Circulation, Addiction, Tobacco Control, IJE…), prefer meta-analyses, pooled cohorts and large prospective cohorts; WHO (incl. IARC monographs, WHO CVD risk charts, WHO AUDIT manual, WHO fact sheets); US Surgeon General reports (2004, 2010, 2014, 2020 cessation); CDC; NHS; NICE; GBD / IHME papers; Indian government and ICMR sources (GATS-2 India, MoHFW, NCDIR, national quitline); Indian cohort studies (Million Death Study, Mumbai cohort, Kerala / Trivandrum cohort, CURES, etc.).
**Never allowed:** quit-smoking or drinking apps and their marketing numbers (Smoke Free, QuitNow, Kwit…), health blogs, news articles (go to the study they cite), Wikipedia numbers, calculators on commercial sites, AI-generated sites, tobacco / alcohol industry-funded work. If a number is only found there, put it in not_found.

## Rules
- **Open every source first.** Copy numbers exactly as printed, with their confidence interval, and say where they are (table / figure / page / abstract). If only the abstract is readable (paywall), use the abstract and write "abstract only".
- **One model = one source**, or a combination the source itself makes. Don't stitch coefficients from different papers into a new formula yourself; if you think a combination is needed, describe it in "caveats" and we'll decide.
- **Say who was studied:** country, men / women, age range, years, how exposure was measured. Flag clearly whether it was validated in South Asians / Indians. Prefer Indian or South Asian data when it exists; give the Western model too if it's better established.
- **Say the valid range:** e.g. only ages 40–74, only up to 40 cigarettes a day. We will not extrapolate outside it.
- **Dose-response is what we need most:** risk at 1–4, 5–9, 10–19, 20+ cigarettes a day (or per pack-year), per 10 g alcohol a day, etc., not just "smokers vs non-smokers". If a paper gives a curve, give its equation or the table of points.
- **Bidi, hookah, vape and smokeless tobacco are different products:** find their own numbers; if only "equivalent to N cigarettes" claims exist, give the source and how it was measured. If evidence is weak or missing (e.g. long-term vaping), say so plainly; "not enough evidence" is a valid answer.
- **Separate relative and absolute risk,** and current vs former smokers.
- **Never invent a "lung health %", "body score" or similar.** If no validated composite exists for our inputs, say so.
- Units: grams of ethanol (not "drinks") for alcohol; if a source uses drinks, give its drink size in grams.
- For each model, add one plain line of app copy (user_copy) in simple English: "about", the range, no shame, no diagnosis, no "you will die" language.
